# Flight bookings module

Flight bookings (`flight_bookings`). The flights and their cabin seat counters belong to the flights module, and this module reaches them only through `FlightSeatHoldsService` (see "Seat holds (FLT-22)" in the flights README).

## Hold seats for payment (FLT-22)

| Route                            | Requirement                            | Access                        |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| `POST /api/flight-bookings/hold` | FLT-22, FLT-19, FLT-12 (GEN-11 status) | Registered user (role `user`) |
| `POST /api/flight-bookings`      | FLT-21 (summary), FLT-12               | Registered user (role `user`) |

- **Who may call it.** `@Roles(Role.User)`: guests get 401, administrators and moderators 403.
- **The body** is `flightId`, `cabin` (`economy`, `premium_economy`, `business`, `first`) and `passengers`: the list of every passenger with their details and optional extra bags (FLT-19, see below). The client never sends a passenger type, price, fee, status, hold time, owner, seat count or reference: `forbidNonWhitelisted` answers 400 for any of them, also inside a passenger.
- **Passenger rules** (FLT-12(b)), on the types the server derives: at least one adult, and no more infants than adults. Adults and children take a seat, infants do not, so `seatsReserved = adults + children`.
- **At most 9 seats per booking** (`FLIGHT_BOOKING_SEATS_MAX`). The workbook sets no maximum; 9 is our limit, the usual airline limit for one booking, and it keeps one request from emptying a cabin. A booking above it gets 400 on `passengers`.
- **One route: this call is "proceeds to payment".** Section 8 of the project description has `POST /api/flight-bookings` for FLT-19 and FLT-21 and `POST /api/flight-bookings/hold` for FLT-22. The team leader decided: FLT-19 adds the passengers' details and the baggage to this `/hold` body, and no `POST /api/flight-bookings` was added then. FLT-21 now uses `POST /api/flight-bookings` for the summary before the hold, with the same body (see "Booking summary (FLT-21)").
- **Read, check, hold, verify, save.** The FLT-19 checks run before the hold (see "Passenger details and extra baggage" below). One `now` per request. `FlightSeatHoldsService.hold` reserves the seats with one conditional update on the flight (scheduled at `now`, cabin present, enough seats left) and returns the flight as the update left it. The booking is then saved as `pending_payment` with `holdExpiresAt = now + 10 minutes` (`FLIGHT_BOOKING_HOLD_MINUTES`) and a pre-made `_id`. If saving fails, the seats are released and the save error is rethrown, so nothing stays held for a booking that does not exist. If that release fails too, it is logged and the counter stays one booking too high until recomputed.
- **Why the order is hold then save.** Saving first would leave a `pending_payment` booking without seats when the hold is refused; holding first leaves at most a counter to give back, which the service does at once.
- **No transaction.** The CI database is a single MongoDB server, which has none, and none is needed: the seat rule is the filter of one update on one flight document, which MongoDB applies atomically. The e2e test sends 6 one-seat requests at once for a 3-seat cabin and gets exactly three 201 and three 409, `heldSeats` 3 and 3 bookings.
- **Price.** `fare` is the cabin price read from the flight document **returned by the same update** that held the seats, not from an earlier read, so a price edit at the same moment (FLT-05) cannot give a total that differs from the price the seats were taken at. `fareTotal = fare × seats + infants × Math.round(fare × 10 / 100)`: an infant pays 10% of the fare (FLT-12), rounded to a whole minor unit. `total = fareTotal + baggageTotal` (FLT-19). Money is whole minor units of `PLATFORM_CURRENCY` (USD, PAY-04).
- **Errors.** 400 for the body and the FLT-19 checks, 404 "Flight not found" (also for a flight of a deactivated airline, ACC-15: a deactivated provider is hidden and not bookable), 409 "This flight can no longer be booked" (cancelled or departed, or its departure time has passed, FLT-30), 404 "This flight has no business cabin" (the cabin sent is named), 409 "Not enough seats left in this cabin", 409 "The flight changed while booking. Try again." (FLT-19). Nothing stays held in any error case.
- **Response** (`FlightBookingResponseDto`, `toFlightBookingResponse`): `id`, `status`, `holdExpiresAt` (UTC, ending in `Z`), `currency`, `fareTotal`, `baggageTotal`, `total`, `segments: [{ flightId, cabin, fare, seatsReserved }]`, `passengerCounts`, `passengers: [{ fullName, dateOfBirth, type, passportNumber, extraBags }]`, `createdAt`. Only the owner gets it (the hold answers its own caller). Never the owner id, the segment's airline id, the copied policy, the flight's seat counters or Mongo internals. `reference` is left out until the payment gives one.

## Passenger details and extra baggage (FLT-19)

FLT-19: "enter each passenger's full name, date of birth and passport/ID number and optionally add extra baggage (bags and weight) per passenger up to the airline's maximum, with any baggage fee added to the booking total." FLT-12(b): "the type is validated against the date of birth (FLT-19)". The design was decided by the team leader; the reasons are below.

- **One route.** The details go into the `POST /api/flight-bookings/hold` body: `passengers: [{ fullName, dateOfBirth, passportNumber, extraBags? { bags, weightKg } }]` (`PassengerDetailsDto`, `ExtraBagsDto`). This replaces the `{ adults, children, infants }` counts of FLT-22. No new route was added by FLT-19; FLT-21 then gave `POST /api/flight-bookings` the summary, with this same body.
- **Derived types, never sent.** The client sends no type (a `type` field is 400). The server derives it from the date of birth **on the flight's local departure date** (`departureDateLocal`), because FLT-12(b) defines the types by age and the age that counts is the one at the flight, not at booking: adult 12 or older, child 2 to 11, infant younger than 2 (`passengerTypeOn`). The age is whole years between two `YYYY-MM-DD` dates, so no time zone is involved; the birthday is reached on the same month and day, and a 29 February birthday is reached on 1 March in a common year. Then the FLT-12 rules apply to the derived types, and `passengerCounts` keeps the derived counts.
- **Field rules (400 with the field path, such as `passengers.1.dateOfBirth`):** `fullName` trimmed, 2 to 100 characters; `dateOfBirth` a real `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date (29 February only in a leap year), not after today (UTC), not more than 120 years back, and not after the flight's local departure date (today in UTC can be one day after a local departure west of UTC); `passportNumber` trimmed and upper-cased, 5 to 20 letters and digits; 1 to 18 passengers (at most 9 adults and children plus at most as many infants as adults). The same passport number twice in one booking is 400 on the second one (`passengers.N.passportNumber`), compared after upper-casing.
- **Our own limits.** The workbook gives no name length, passport format or maximum age. `PASSENGER_FULL_NAME_MIN_LENGTH`/`MAX_LENGTH` (2, 100), `PASSPORT_NUMBER_MIN_LENGTH`/`MAX_LENGTH` (5, 20) and `PASSENGER_MAX_AGE_YEARS` (120) in `flight-bookings.constants.ts` are ours, and `FLIGHT_BOOKING_PASSENGERS_MAX` (18) follows from the two FLT-12 rules.
- **Extra bags, how we read FLT-08.** Per passenger, `bags` from 1 to the airline's `maxExtraBags`, each at the airline's `extraBagPrice`; the fee is `bags × extraBagPrice`. An airline that sells no extra bag (no price, or `maxExtraBags` 0) answers 400 on that passenger's `extraBags`; more than its maximum is 400 on `extraBags.bags`. FLT-08 says each extra bag has the cabin's maximum weight: `weightKg` is the **total** weight of that passenger's extra bags, so it must be at most `bags × maxBagKg` of the booked cabin (400 on `extraBags.weightKg`). Infants may have extra bags like anyone else: the workbook makes no exception. The price is copied into the segment (`segment.extraBagPrice`, done at hold time since PAY-02), and each passenger keeps `baggage: [{ segment: 0, bags, weightKg }]`, as in the data model (FLT-12 adds segment 1 for the return flight).
- **Money.** `baggageTotal` is the sum of the fees and `total = fareTotal + baggageTotal`, all whole minor units. The fare still comes from the flight document returned by the hold (FLT-22).
- **Order: read, check, hold, verify, save.** The types need the local departure date, and the weight rule needs the cabin's `maxBagKg`, before the seat count is known. So:
  1. one light read of the flight and a read of the airline (`planBooking`, shared with the summary since FLT-21, which widened the read to `PLANNED_FLIGHT_FIELDS` so the summary can show the flight). A missing flight or airline, a deactivated airline (ACC-15), a flight that can no longer be booked (FLT-30) and a missing cabin get the hold's own 404 or 409 here, before any 400, so a hidden airline never shows its bag rule in a 400 (found by the reviewer);
  2. `planPassengers` derives the types, checks every rule and prices the bags; any error is a 400 and nothing is held;
  3. `FlightSeatHoldsService.hold` holds adults + children;
  4. the flight the hold returned must still have the same `departureDateLocal` and cabin `maxBagKg`: if a moderator edited them in between (FLT-05, FLT-04), the seats are released and the answer is 409 "The flight changed while booking. Try again.";
  5. the booking is saved; if that fails, the seats are released (FLT-22).
- **The airline is now read before the hold** (it was after the hold in PAY-02), because the extra-bag rule is needed for the checks. A failed airline read now holds nothing at all, instead of releasing seats afterwards.
- **One scope note.** The flights module exports no way to read a flight's local departure date, and this issue stays in this module. So `FlightBookingsModule` registers the `Flight` schema for **one read-only, `select`-limited query**; the seats are still changed only through `FlightSeatHoldsService`. `HeldFlight` names only the fields FLT-22 needed, so the service reads `departureDateLocal` from the returned document as optional, and a missing date counts as changed (409, seats released). The cleaner long-term version is a method on `FlightSeatHoldsService` and `departureDateLocal` in `HeldFlight`: a small flights-module change for its owner.
- **Personal data.** Names, dates of birth and passport numbers are returned only to the owner, never logged (the only log of the hold has the booking id and the seat count), and no validation message repeats a value (DTO and schema messages are fixed texts). The e2e test checks that none of them reaches stdout or stderr.
- **What later issues added.** FLT-12 added the return segment (see "Round trip (FLT-12)" below). FLT-21 shows the summary (flight, passengers, baggage, total) before payment at `POST /api/flight-bookings`.

## Booking summary (FLT-21)

FLT-21: "The system shall show a booking summary (flight, passengers, baggage, total) before payment." `POST /api/flight-bookings` returns it (200, `FlightBookingSummaryResponseDto`); the user then calls `POST /api/flight-bookings/hold` to proceed to payment.

- **The route.** Section 8 gives `POST /api/flight-bookings` to FLT-19 and FLT-21; FLT-19 put the passengers on `/hold`, so this route is the summary. `@Roles(Role.User)` like the hold: a guest gets 401 and the frontend sends them to log in and back (ACC-10); administrators and moderators get 403. It answers **200, not 201**, because nothing is created.
- **Why POST with the same body.** The summary needs the passengers (types, fares, bag fees), and names, dates of birth and passport numbers must never go into a URL or a query string. The body is `HoldFlightBookingDto` exactly, so the frontend sends the same body twice: once for the summary, once for the hold.
- **One planning step and one price, shared with the hold.** `planBooking(dto, now)` runs the hold's checks in the hold's order with the hold's messages (404 "Flight not found", including a deactivated airline; 409 "This flight can no longer be booked"; 404 for a missing cabin; then the 400s of `planPassengers`). `priceBooking(fare, plan)` (`booking-price.ts`) computes `fareTotal`, `baggageTotal`, `total` and each passenger's fare and bag fee, for the summary with the cabin price it read and for the hold with the price of the flight its update returned. So the summary and the hold cannot disagree for the same body and the same cabin price; a unit test and the e2e test check it.
- **Nothing held, nothing saved.** The summary reads the flight once (`PLANNED_FLIGHT_FIELDS`) and the airline once, and writes nothing: no seat counter changes and no `flight_bookings` document. The e2e test reads the cabin's `heldSeats` and counts the user's bookings of the flight afterwards.
- **The seat check only informs.** When the cabin already has fewer seats left (`totalSeats - bookedSeats - heldSeats`) than adults + children, the answer is 409 "Not enough seats left in this cabin", the hold's own message. A 200 promises nothing: someone else can take the seats, or the airline can change the price, before the hold, which checks the seats again atomically and takes the price of that moment. The response says so in `priceNote` ("Prices and seats are confirmed when you proceed to payment (POST /api/flight-bookings/hold).").
- **The fields.** `flight` (the flight as the API returns it, with the GEN-06 local times, from `FlightResponsesService.toResponse`), `airline { id, name, code }`, `cabin`, `seats`, `passengerCounts`, `passengers [{ fullName, dateOfBirth, type, passportNumber, fare, extraBags (or null), baggageFee }]` in the order of the request, `baggage { freeBags, maxBagKg, extraBagPrice (null when the airline sells none), maxExtraBags }`, `currency`, `fareTotal`, `baggageTotal`, `total`, `priceNote`. No id, status, hold time, reference, owner id, seat counters or Mongo internals: nothing was saved. The cancellation tiers are not shown here: they are shown and accepted after the hold (PAY-02, `GET /api/bookings/flight/:id/policies`).
- **Personal data.** Returned only to the caller, never logged; the e2e test checks that no name, date of birth or passport number reaches stdout or stderr.
- **The hold did not change.** `hold()` now calls `planBooking` and `priceBooking`; its checks, their order, its messages and its response are the same, and the hold and passengers e2e suites pass unchanged.
- **Tests:** `booking-price.spec.ts`, the `summarise` cases of `flight-bookings.service.spec.ts` (each refusal, the seat check, no hold and no save, the same totals as the hold), `mappers/flight-booking-summary.mapper.spec.ts`, `flight-bookings.controller.spec.ts` (roles, 200, path) and `test/flight-bookings-summary.e2e-spec.ts`.

## Round trip (FLT-12)

FLT-12(a): "round-trip search returning outbound and return options, booked as one booking with two segments, one payment and one reference, and cancelled as a whole using the outbound segment's tier." The search half is in the flights README ("Round trip and cabin filter"); the booking half is here. (b), the passenger types, came with FLT-19 above.

- **The body.** `POST /api/flight-bookings/hold` and the summary take three optional fields: `returnFlightId` and `returnCabin` (both or neither: 400 "returnCabin is required with returnFlightId" / "returnFlightId is required with returnCabin"; null counts as left out), and per passenger `returnExtraBags` (same shape and rules as `extraBags`). Without them the booking is one-way and nothing changed. `returnExtraBags` on a one-way booking is 400 on that passenger. The return cabin may differ from the outbound one. Swagger shows a `roundTrip` example next to `oneAdult`.
- **One booking, two segments.** A round trip is saved as one `flight_bookings` document with `segments: [outbound, return]` (the data model's `segment` 0 and 1), so it has one `holdExpiresAt`, one payment (PAY-01) and one reference (FLT-23). Each segment has its own flight, airline, cabin, fare and copied policy (PAY-02): the cancellation tiers, extra-bag price and maximum of its airline and the free bags and bag weight of its cabin, because the two directions can be different airlines. Each passenger is stored once, with `baggage` entries for `segment: 0` and `segment: 1`. `fareTotal`, `baggageTotal` and `total` cover both flights.
- **The round-trip rules (400 on `returnFlightId`, in the same answer as the passenger 400s).** The return flight must fly the outbound route reversed (from the outbound arrival airport to its departure airport; "The return flight must fly from JFK to CAI, the outbound route reversed") and must leave after the outbound flight arrives (strictly after `arrivalAt`, compared as UTC instants; "The return flight must leave after the outbound flight arrives"). The workbook gives no minimum connection time, so one minute is enough.
- **The return flight's own answers come first and say so.** Both flights are read and checked like a one-way flight (missing flight or airline, deactivated airline, not bookable, missing cabin) before any 400; the return flight's 404 and 409 are the same answers prefixed "Return flight: " (`forReturnFlight`), for example "Return flight: Not enough seats left in this cabin", so the user knows which flight to change. Any other error passes unchanged.
- **Passenger types on a round trip.** A booking stores one type per passenger, so the type is the one of the **outbound** date (FLT-12(b)) and the seats are the same on both flights. A child who turns 12 before the return stays a child: the same seat and fare. An infant who turns 2 before (or on) the return date would need a seat on the return flight, so that round trip is 400 on the passenger's `dateOfBirth` ("…book two one-way trips"). This keeps one type and one seat count per booking, as the data model has them.
- **Bags of each flight.** `extraBags` are checked and priced with the outbound airline and cabin, `returnExtraBags` with the return airline and cabin (`planReturnPassengers`, which shares `feeOfExtraBags` with `planPassengers`), with the error paths `passengers.N.returnExtraBags…`.
- **Read, check, hold both, verify, save.** `planBooking` reads both flights and airlines and runs every check before anything is held. The hold then holds the outbound seats, then the return seats, each with the one conditional update of FLT-22 at the same `now`. If the return hold is refused, the outbound seats are released before the error (prefixed) is rethrown. After both holds, each returned flight must still have its departure date and bag weight (FLT-19), **and the return must still leave after the outbound arrives** (a moderator may move the times between the read and the hold, FLT-05); otherwise 409 "The flight changed while booking. Try again." Any error after a hold releases every seat held so far; a failed release is logged with the flight id and the seat count only. Concurrent round trips never hold more seats than a cabin has, on either flight (tested: 5 at once for a 2-seat return cabin give 2 bookings, and the 3 refused give their outbound seat back).
- **The summary (FLT-21)** runs the same `planBooking` and price function. The top-level `flight`, `airline`, `cabin`, `passengers` and `baggage` are the outbound flight, `returnTrip` has the return flight, its airline, its cabin, what each passenger pays on it and its bag rule (`null` one-way), and `fareTotal`, `baggageTotal` and `total` cover both flights: the totals the hold charges for the same body and prices. The informative seat check covers the return cabin too ("Return flight: Not enough seats left in this cabin").
- **The response.** `segments` lists both flights, outbound first; each passenger has `extraBags` (segment 0) and `returnExtraBags` (segment 1, `null` one-way).
- **Expiry and confirmation.** The expiry job releases the seats of every segment, so a round trip expires as one booking, and the confirmation (FLT-23) moves the held seats to booked on both segments' flights (both tested).
- **Cancelled as a whole, with the outbound tiers.** `refundTiersOf(booking)` (`booking-refund.ts`) gives the tiers of the first segment, as copied at hold time, for the whole booking; the return segment's tiers are kept for the record but never decide a refund. The cancellation itself (FLT-26) is not built yet: it must cancel both segments and use `refundTiersOf`. The policies route (PAY-02) still lists each segment's tiers as stored; showing only the outbound tiers there is a small follow-up for the payments module.
- **Tests:** `flight-bookings-round-trip.service.spec.ts` (the order of reads and holds, both segments and their policies, the totals, the bags per segment, the fare of each returned flight, every release path, a changed return flight and changed times, the return flight's prefixed 404s and 409s, every 400 alone and together, one-way unchanged, the summary with the same totals, `returnLeavesAfterArrival`, `forReturnFlight`), `booking-passengers.spec.ts` (`planReturnPassengers` on the exact second birthday and the day after, a child who becomes an adult, the return bag rules; `returnBagsWithoutReturnFlight`), `dto/hold-flight-booking.dto.spec.ts` (the pair rule, ids, cabins, the return bags, server fields), `booking-refund.spec.ts`, the mapper specs and `test/flight-bookings-round-trip.e2e-spec.ts` (on the `-test` database: the hold with two airlines, the seats on both flights, the stored policies and refund tiers, the policies route, the expiry of both, the summary with the hold's totals, every refusal of the return flight with nothing left held, 5 concurrent holds for 2 return seats, every 400, a guest, Swagger).

## The booking document

- `FlightBooking` follows `FLIGHT_BOOKING` of the data model: `userId`, `status` (`FlightBookingStatus`, the five GEN-11 values shared with hotel reservations), `holdExpiresAt` (required while `pending_payment`), `currency`, `fareTotal`, `baggageTotal` (FLT-19, default 0), `total`, `segments`, `passengerCounts`, `passengers` (FLT-19: `fullName`, `dateOfBirth` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` text never a `Date`, `passportNumber`, `type`, `baggage: [{ segment, bags, weightKg }]`), `policyAcceptedAt` (PAY-02, set once when the user accepts the policies), `reference` and the timestamps. A booking saved before FLT-19 has no passengers and a `baggageTotal` of 0; the mapper shows `passengers: []` for it. Money fields are validated as whole numbers of 0 or more (`fare` at least 1, like the cabin price).
- `segments` holds one segment for a one-way booking and two for a round trip (FLT-12: outbound, then return), each with `flightId`, `airlineId`, `cabin`, `fare` and `seatsReserved`. The flight times are always read from the flight.
- **The airline's policy is copied into each segment at hold time (PAY-02):** `cancellationTiers` (highest `daysBefore` first, empty means no refund), `extraBagPrice` (unset when the airline sells no extra bags), `maxExtraBags` (0 when the airline has no rule), and the held cabin's `freeBags` and `maxBagKg`. `FlightBookingsService.hold` reads the airline (`AirlinesService.findEntityOrThrow`) before the seats are held (FLT-19 needs the extra-bag rule for its checks), so a failed read holds nothing. Why: what the user is shown and accepts before payment is exactly what a refund (FLT-26) and the extra bags (FLT-19) use, whatever the airline changes later, as hotel reservations already do. A booking saved before PAY-02 has no copy: the policies route shows empty tiers, `extraBagPrice` `null`, `maxExtraBags` 0 and `freeBags`/`maxBagKg` `null`, and never reads the live airline for it.
- **For the payments module (PAY-02):** `findOwnedEntityOrThrow(id, userId)` (404 "Booking not found" for a missing booking or another user's, ACC-11) and `acceptPolicies(id, userId, now)` (one conditional update: owner, `pending_payment`, `holdExpiresAt > now`, not accepted before; `null` when nothing matched). `FlightBookingsModule` exports `FlightBookingsService` for them and never imports the payments module.
- **For the admin module (ACC-15):** `findTravellerIdsOfAirline(airlineId, now)`, read only: the distinct user ids with a `confirmed` booking that has a segment on a flight of the airline departing after `now`, used to notify the travellers when the airline is deactivated.
- `passengerCounts` keeps how many adults, children and infants the booking is for, derived from `passengers` (FLT-19), because infants have no seat but pay.
- `reference` stays empty until the confirmation sets it (FLT-23), so its unique index is `sparse`.
- Indexes from the spec: unique sparse `reference`; (`userId`, `status`) for "my bookings" (FLT-25); `segments.flightId` for the bookings of a flight (FLT-06, FLT-29); (`status`, `holdExpiresAt`) for the expiry job.

## Expiry of unpaid holds (FLT-22, GEN-11)

- `FlightBookingExpiryService` runs every minute (`@Cron(CronExpression.EVERY_MINUTE)`, with `ScheduleModule.forRoot()` registered once in `AppModule` for both flight and hotel holds). The scheduled method catches and logs every error: a job must never crash the app.
- `expireDueHolds(now)` reads the `pending_payment` bookings whose `holdExpiresAt <= now`, oldest first, at most 100 per run (`FLIGHT_BOOKING_EXPIRY_BATCH_SIZE`), through the (`status`, `holdExpiresAt`) index. For each one it sets `status: expired` with an update that matches `{ _id, status: pending_payment, holdExpiresAt: { $lte: now } }`, and **only when that update matched** it releases the seats of each segment through `FlightSeatHoldsService.release`.
- **Expire first, then release.** A booking confirmed in between does not match, so its seats are never released; a booking another run already expired does not match, so its seats are never released twice. A booking can therefore never be both confirmed and released. If a release fails after the expire, the error is logged and `heldSeats` stays one booking too high until it is recomputed: the counters are caches that can always be recomputed from the bookings (spec, "Conventions"). One failing booking does not stop the others.
- **Up to one minute late.** Between the end of a hold and the next run, the seats still count as held. This only delays seats becoming free again; it never lets more seats be sold than exist.
- The e2e test calls `expireDueHolds` directly instead of waiting for the cron.

## Confirmation after payment (FLT-23, PAY-01)

| Route                                   | Requirement | Access                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `GET /api/flight-bookings/:id/e-ticket` | FLT-23      | Registered user (role `user`), own booking |

- **Who calls it.** The payments webhook (PAY-01), once Stripe reports that the money arrived and the amount matches. `FlightBookingsService.confirmPaid(bookingId, now)` is the `BookingPaymentHandler` that `PaymentsModule` finds on this service; it delegates to `FlightBookingConfirmationService`. No route confirms a booking: the browser never decides.
- **Order: confirm, then book the seats, then notify.**
  1. **Confirm** with one `findOneAndUpdate` that matches `{ _id, status: pending_payment, holdExpiresAt: { $gt: now } }` and sets `status: confirmed` and the `reference`. The expiry job matches `holdExpiresAt <= now`, so the two can never both win: a booking is never both confirmed and released.
  2. **Book the seats** of each segment (both flights of a round trip) through `FlightSeatHoldsService.confirm`: one update on the flight that moves `seatsReserved` from the cabin's `heldSeats` to `bookedSeats`, guarded by `heldSeats >= seats`. The seats left (`totalSeats - bookedSeats - heldSeats`) do not change. Infants occupy no seat, so the count is `seatsReserved`, as in the hold.
  3. **Notify** the owner (GEN-01, `booking_confirmed`, linked to the `flight_booking`).
- **Why this order.** The confirmation is the user's proof of payment, so it is saved first and never undone. If moving the seats fails or does not match afterwards, an error with the booking id and the seat count is logged and the seats stay counted as **held**: that is the safe direction (nothing can be oversold), and the counters are caches that can be recomputed from the bookings. The notification is sent after the confirmation; if reading the flights or sending fails, it is logged with the booking id only (never a name or passport) and the booking stays confirmed, like the notification of a flight edit (FLT-05).
- **Idempotent.** The same payment event can arrive twice. When the update does not match, the booking is read: a booking with a reference was confirmed before (it keeps the reference when it is completed or cancelled later), so the answer is `true` and nothing changes, with no second notification.
- **A late payment is not confirmed.** A booking whose hold had ended (or is expired, or no longer exists) answers `false` without throwing, so Stripe gets 200 and the payments module logs it for the refund (PAY-03). A database error throws, so the webhook answers 500 and Stripe retries.
- **The reference** looks like `FB-7KQ2MX`: `FB-` (flight booking, so it is never mixed up with a hotel one) and 6 characters from `23456789ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTUVWXYZ`, without the look-alikes 0/O and 1/I/L, drawn with `crypto.randomInt` (about 887 million values, not guessable from another one). The unique sparse index on `reference` decides uniqueness: on a duplicate key the confirming update is tried again with a new reference, at most 5 times (`REFERENCE_ATTEMPTS`), then the error is thrown and Stripe retries the event.
- **The notification** names the reference and, for each flight, the flight number, the route (airport codes), the local departure time at its airport (luxon with the airport's IANA zone, GEN-06), the cabin and the seats, for example "Your flight booking FB-7KQ2MX is confirmed. MS123 CAI to JFK, departs 2027-06-15 14:30 CAI time, Economy, 2 seats." A round trip adds "Return: …"; infants are mentioned because they have no seat.
- **The e-ticket** (`GET /api/flight-bookings/:id/e-ticket`) is a PDF made with pdfkit (`e-ticket/e-ticket-pdf.ts`), sent with `Content-Type: application/pdf` and `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="e-ticket-<reference>.pdf"`. Only the owner gets it (another user's booking is 404, ACC-11) and only once it is `confirmed` (409 "Only a confirmed booking has an e-ticket" while pending, and when expired, cancelled or completed). It shows the reference; for each flight the airline, flight number, route, the local departure and arrival with their IANA zone and UTC offset, the cabin, the seats and the free baggage copied at hold time; each passenger's name, type and **masked passport number** (5 stars and the last 3 characters, so not even its length shows) with their extra bags per flight; and the total paid in USD from whole cents. Nothing about the passengers is logged. The built-in Helvetica font covers Western European letters only, so a name in another script prints wrongly on the PDF (the stored data is correct).
- **No confirmation time field.** The data model's `FLIGHT_BOOKING` has none, so none was added; `updatedAt` changes with the confirmation.
